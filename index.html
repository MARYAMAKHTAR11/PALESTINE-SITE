Stand With Palestine: Remembering History, Confronting Reality, Supporting Resistance.

A comprehensive guide to understanding Gaza’s history, current struggles, and actionable steps every human and Muslim can take today.

A history that did not begin in 2023

Gaza’s story is part of a longer one: a people, a homeland, and more than seven decades of displacement and military rule.

Before 1948

An indigenous society with deep roots

For centuries Palestine was home to a majority Arab population of Muslims and Christians, alongside a Jewish community, living in towns such as Jaffa, Haifa, Nablus, Hebron, Jerusalem and Gaza. Palestinians farmed olive groves and citrus orchards, traded across the Mediterranean, and built a rich culture of poetry, cuisine, embroidery and scholarship. After the 1917 Balfour Declaration and the British Mandate (1920–1948), rising Zionist immigration and land purchases brought growing conflict over the country’s future.

1948

The Nakba (“the Catastrophe”)

During the war that followed the 1947 UN partition plan and the creation of Israel, around 750,000 Palestinians fled or were expelled from their homes, according to UN estimates. Hundreds of Palestinian villages were depopulated and many were destroyed. Gaza, a small coastal strip, absorbed a huge wave of refugees, and today most of its people are refugees or their descendants. UN General Assembly Resolution 194 affirmed the refugees’ right to return, a right that has never been implemented.

1967

The Six-Day War and occupation

Israel captured the West Bank, East Jerusalem and the Gaza Strip in June 1967. UN Security Council Resolution 242 called for Israeli withdrawal from territories occupied in the conflict. Instead, Israel built settlements on occupied land. In July 2024 the International Court of Justice issued an advisory opinion that Israel’s continued presence in the occupied Palestinian territory is unlawful and that settlements violate international law.

2007 – present

The siege of Gaza

Israel withdrew its settlers and troops from inside Gaza in 2005. After Hamas took control of the Strip in 2007, Israel (with Egypt’s cooperation at the Rafah crossing) imposed a land, sea and air blockade that restricted the movement of people, goods, fuel and building materials. Human rights groups and UN bodies described it as collective punishment, and Gaza’s economy, water supply and health system were weakened for years before the war that began in October 2023.

Gaza today: the current reality

A ceasefire took effect on 10 October 2025, yet the humanitarian emergency continues. The figures below come from named organisations and may change.

74,254+

The human cost

Gaza’s Health Ministry reports at least this many Palestinians killed since October 2023, with over 1,400 more killed after the ceasefire began. UNICEF reported at least 300 children killed in the first 300 days of the truce. Entire families, neighbourhoods and generations have been lost, and nearly all of Gaza’s population has been displaced at least once.

Sources: Gaza Health Ministry; Islamic Relief (Oct 2026); UNICEF (Aug 2026)
~2.1 million

Infrastructure crisis

Islamic Relief reports that about 2.1 million people are squeezed into roughly 35% of the territory. UN agencies and the WHO have documented severe damage to hospitals, schools, universities, homes and water and sanitation systems, leaving children facing malnutrition, disease and limited access to clean water and education.

Sources: Islamic Relief; UNICEF; WHO and OCHA reporting
Sabr

Resilience and faith

Through bombardment and loss, people in Gaza have kept teaching children in tents, running field clinics, baking bread on open fires, and welcoming the displaced. Their patience (sabr) and trust in God, expressed in the phrase “Hasbunallahu wa ni’mal wakeel,” have become a source of strength for Muslims and for people of conscience worldwide.

Reported by journalists and aid workers on the ground

Documenting Israeli policies and military conduct

Courts, UN bodies and major human rights organisations have made serious findings against Israeli policy. These are summarised here as findings and allegations, with their sources.

🏚️

Occupation and settlements

The ICJ ruled in 2024 that the occupation is unlawful and that settlements breach the Fourth Geneva Convention. Settlers and settlement infrastructure continue to expand across the West Bank and East Jerusalem, with home demolitions and settler violence documented by the UN.

⚖️

Apartheid and genocide findings

Amnesty International, Human Rights Watch and Israel’s own B’Tselem have described a system of apartheid. Amnesty and a UN Commission of Inquiry have concluded that Israel committed genocide in Gaza. In 2024 the ICJ ordered provisional measures in South Africa v. Israel, and the ICC issued arrest warrants for Israeli leaders.

🩺

Civilians, medics and aid

The UN and the WHO have recorded hundreds of attacks on health facilities and the deaths of large numbers of medical workers. UN agencies report record numbers of aid workers killed, and aid deliveries repeatedly obstructed. UNRWA has been banned by Israeli law, and famine conditions were confirmed in parts of Gaza in 2025.

📰

Journalists

The Committee to Protect Journalists has described this as the deadliest conflict for journalists it has recorded. Foreign reporters have largely been barred from entering Gaza independently, so Palestinian journalists have carried the burden of documenting events.

Israel’s position: The Israeli government rejects the charge of genocide and apartheid. It says it is acting in self-defence after the 7 October 2023 Hamas-led attack, in which about 1,200 people were killed and around 250 taken hostage, and that Hamas embeds fighters among civilians. These disputes are before international courts, and readers are encouraged to read the primary sources themselves.

How Gaza can survive, and how we can help

Survival depends on aid reaching people, on the killing stopping, and on the world refusing to look away. Sumud, steadfast perseverance, means staying rooted in land, family, education and faith. Our responsibility is to stand behind it.

🤲

Make continuous dua

The Prophet ﷺ taught that the supplication of the oppressed is answered. Pray in every salah, in the last third of the night, and teach children to pray for Palestine by name.

📣

Spread awareness

Learn the history, then explain it to family, classmates and colleagues. Share verified reports from UN agencies, courts and credible journalists, and check before you forward.

🤝

Maintain unity

Set aside sectarian and political quarrels. The Quran calls the believers one brotherhood, and unity makes every other effort stronger. Reject hatred of any people: our cause is justice, not enmity toward Jews or any faith.

🕊️

Peaceful protest

Join lawful marches, vigils and community events. Keep them peaceful, respectful and free from abuse, so the message stays about human rights.

📱

Truth-sharing online

Amplify named, verifiable sources, share first-hand voices from Gaza, and report disinformation. Always keep the dignity of victims in mind.

✉️

Write to representatives

Contact your MNA, senator, MP or local councillor. Ask for an end to arms transfers, support for humanitarian access, and compliance with ICJ and ICC rulings.

Boycott guide: the BDS movement

BDS (Boycott, Divestment, Sanctions) was launched in 2005 by Palestinian civil society. Supporters see it as a peaceful, nonviolent tool inspired by the campaign against apartheid in South Africa: ordinary people use their spending to stop supporting what they see as injustice. Critics argue that boycotts are unfair or ineffective, and some governments disagree with the campaign. Inclusion below reflects what BDS and consumer campaigns call for, not a legal ruling.

Why boycott works

It is legal, peaceful and open to everyone. It raises the reputational and financial cost of doing business with a policy, it keeps attention on the issue, and it needs no leader or budget, just a million small decisions.

✅

Before you boycott

Brand lists change and franchise ownership varies by country. Check the current guidance at bdsmovement.net, avoid harassing local shop workers, and remember that many staff are your neighbours.
